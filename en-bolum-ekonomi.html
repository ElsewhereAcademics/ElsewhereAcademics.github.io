Economics

Economics teaches the theoretical and quantitative tools needed to understand economic systems, financial markets and social policy. Besides microeconomics and macroeconomics, mathematics, statistics and econometrics form the core of the program.

IN HIGH SCHOOL

Which subjects matter?

For September 2027 entry to Economics BSc (Econ), UCL asks for A* in mathematics at A-level and 7 in Higher Level mathematics in the IB. LSE also requires A-level mathematics for BSc Economics and counts Further Mathematics as a plus.

Exams required for this field

TMUA United Kingdom

For the 2026 intake, UCL Economics BSc (Econ) requires the TMUA from all applicants. Registration and fees are on the UAT-UK website.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you study economics in English in the Netherlands?

Yes. Erasmus School of Economics' International Bachelor Economics and Business Economics (IBEB) program is in English and takes 3 years. Applicants with A-levels need at least a B in mathematics. For 2027/28 there are 500 places, and the application deadline is 15 January.

Netherlands page Ask your question on WhatsApp
Do you need to take a separate exam for economics in the United Kingdom?

Some schools ask for the TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admission). According to the exam's official website, seven universities use this test for some courses in mathematics, economics or computer science. Cambridge, LSE, UCL, Warwick and Durham are among them. The test has two parts of 75 minutes each. Whether it is required depends on the course, so you need to check each program's own page.

United Kingdom page Ask your question on WhatsApp
Can you study economics together with mathematics?

Yes. In LSE's BSc Mathematics and Economics, the course load is split equally between mathematics and economics, and the program takes 3 years. Applicants with A-levels are normally asked for A*AA, and an A* in mathematics is required. If the school offers Further Mathematics, you are expected to take it too.
