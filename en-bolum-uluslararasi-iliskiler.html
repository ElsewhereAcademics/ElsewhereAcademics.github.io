International Relations

International Relations

International relations studies how power, government, conflict and cooperation shape relations within and between countries. Political theory, global history and global political economy are among the core areas of the program.

Law and social sciences

Example courses

Introduction to international relationsPolitical theoryGlobal historyGlobal political economyRising powers and a changing world

What do graduates do?

  • Diplomat
  • Policy specialist
  • Political risk analyst
  • Intelligence analyst
  • International aid and development specialist
  • Public affairs consultant
  • Public social research specialist
  • Journalist
IN HIGH SCHOOL

Which subjects matter?

LSE sets no specific subject requirement for International Relations (BSc); it prefers at least two traditional academic subjects. Typical choices are history, English, economics, Government and Politics and sociology. It considers a combination of mathematics, further mathematics and just one other subject less competitive.

Example: University College London, first-year courses

  1. International Conflict and Cooperation
  2. Foundations of Political Thought
  3. Democracy & Authoritarianism
  4. Policy Making
  5. Public Sector Economics
  6. Science, Scientific Discovery and Statistics

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is it possible to study international relations in English in Europe?

Yes. Leiden University's International Relations and Organisations bachelor's is fully in English, takes 3 years and is taught in The Hague. The University of Groningen's International Relations and International Organization program is also in English and 180 EC; the school recommends studying abroad for about 16 weeks on average.

Ask your question on WhatsApp
Do these programs in the Netherlands have a limit on places?

It depends on the program. Leiden's International Relations and Organisations program has a numerus fixus, that is, a limit on places. Applications are accepted between 1 October and 15 January, and places are given by selection. Groningen's program only has a non-binding 'program choice check'.
