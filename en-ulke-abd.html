USA

Studying in the USA

01

F-1 visa and boarding school for high school

To study at a high school in the US, the student gets an F-1 visa. With F-1, a student can attend SEVP-approved private schools at any grade level. At a public high school, the stay cannot exceed 12 months in total and the student pays the full cost of the school. At boarding schools such as Phillips Exeter, applications close on 15 January and decisions are announced on 10 March.

02

Applying to universities with one form

Most bachelor's applications are made through Common App. The platform has more than 1,100 member universities. The new application cycle opens on 1 August every year. At Harvard, MIT and Yale, early applications close on 1 November. Early Decision is binding: if you are accepted, the student withdraws their other applications.

03

Choosing a major at the end of the second year

A bachelor's degree usually takes four years full-time, and the academic year runs from August to May. A student can enter a university without choosing a major and often decides at the end of the second year. In the first years, they take courses in English, math, science, humanities and social sciences.

04

Working while studying

F-1 students can work up to 20 hours a week on campus during term. During breaks, full-time work on campus is possible. Working off campus requires a separate permit.

05

OPT after graduation

F-1 students can get up to 12 months of OPT, meaning practical work permission in their field, for each level of education. Graduates in STEM fields can also apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension.

USA

Key points for the USA

01

Early Decision is binding, Early Action is not

With Early Decision, you can apply early to only one university. If you are accepted, the student commits to enrolling at that school and withdraws their other applications. Early Action is not binding. The student has until 1 May to announce their decision.

02

Starting at a community college and transferring to a four-year school

Community colleges are mostly state-funded and offer the first two years of a four-year bachelor's degree. Their fees are usually lower than at four-year schools. After two years, the student earns an Associate of Arts or Associate of Science degree. Some universities accept students into the third year with this degree. Transfer rules vary by school.

IN BRIEF

The USA in brief

High schoolBachelor'sMaster'sGeneral

Last checked: October 2026. Rules and amounts can change. We check the current situation together before you apply.

High school: international students

With an F-1 visa, you can study at private high schools at any grade level and with no time limit. At public high schools, F-1 students' study cannot exceed 12 months in total, and the student must pay the school district the full unsubsidized per-person cost.

Bachelor's: length and application route

A bachelor's degree usually takes four years. Applications are mostly made to each university separately. Common App lets you apply to several universities with one online form. Common App has more than 1,100 member institutions.

Master's: length and application route

A master's degree is usually a two-year degree. MBA programs mostly take one to two years. Graduate applications are usually made to each program separately.

Language of instruction

Universities in the US ask international students for an English proficiency exam result before admission.

Student visa

F-1 student status is used for university and high school study.

Proof of funds

In a student visa application, you must show that you can cover all costs for the length of study. Official sources do not give a fixed amount that applies to everyone.

Working while studying

F-1 students can work up to 20 hours a week on campus during term and full-time during breaks. Off-campus work needs a separate permit.

Work permit after graduation

F-1 students can get up to 12 months of Optional Practical Training (OPT) for each level of education. For graduates in STEM fields, there is a continuous 24-month STEM OPT extension.

Academic calendar

The academic year usually starts in August or September and ends in May.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is the SSAT required at every boarding school?

No, it varies by school. Phillips Exeter asks for the SSAT or ISEE Upper Level from 9th grade applicants. Taft expects the SSAT from 9th and 10th grade applicants. Choate treats test scores as an optional part of the application.

Is an English exam (TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo) required at boarding schools?

Most schools do not require it but strongly recommend it. Taft gives 105 for TOEFL iBT, 7.5 for IELTS and 140 for Duolingo as target scores. Exeter states that the exam is not a required part of the application.

Do you need a student visa for summer school?

It depends on the type of program. The B visitor visa covers only short courses that do not count toward a degree or credit. For credit programs such as the Harvard Secondary School Program, the school issues an I-20 and the student goes through the F-1 process.

How early can we apply for the visa, and when can we travel?

A new student can be issued an F-1 visa up to 365 days before school starts. But you can enter the US with this visa no more than 30 days before the start. The I-901 SEVIS fee of 350 USD must be paid before the interview.

When does the I-20 arrive after acceptance to a boarding school?

The school first waits for the enrollment contract and the deposit. Taft sends the first I-20 by email in early June after these arrive. This leaves time for the visa appointment.
